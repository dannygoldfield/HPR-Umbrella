# Architecture

HPR Umbrella is a modular, human-in-the-loop production system. The HPR Registry
is the operational source of truth. The Candidate Engine coordinates finite
option sets, and media plugins make assets. Human editorial decisions remain
explicit records.

## HPR Registry

The SQLite Registry owns:

- permanent portrait identities that do not contain episode numbers;
- versioned portrait exports and complete metadata provenance;
- visual, audio, and pair candidate records;
- separate reviews, rejection, notes, and selection state;
- approved final masters;
- draft and locked sequence versions;
- publication records created only after a sequence is locked.

JSON manifests remain reproducible provenance records. They are generated by
the system and are not a second database for Danny to maintain.

## Candidate Engine

The Candidate Engine coordinates:

- the 120 archive portraits without assigning their release order;
- deterministic candidate-set planning;
- separate Visual, Audio, Pair, and Publishing banks;
- review status, 1–5 ratings, notes, banking, rejection, and selection;
- unique-audio retirement after final selection;
- replaceable photo sources without losing the creative assembly or sequence history;
- provenance and regeneration instructions.

It does not make artistic selections. A failed pair never implies that its visual or audio component failed.

## Audio Generator

Input:

- immutable audio beds, gestures, and optional music stems;
- an audio recipe and profile;
- a duration and random seed.

Output:

- one loop-ready WAV candidate;
- reproducible ingredient and timing metadata.

The Audio Generator knows nothing about portraits, video, publishing, or analytics.

## Video Generator

Input:

- one finished Registry portrait revision; an optional aligned Lightroom
  development-state set may be used later for calibration research;
- one versioned surface-development recipe and exact field timeline;
- optional film grain or a restrained texture treatment;
- a duration and random seed.

Output:

- one silent 1080 × 1920 MP4 candidate;
- a JSON provenance record.

The Video Generator knows nothing about audio, pairing, publishing, or selection.
Its active direction applies no geometric transform: the crop and facial
structure stay fixed while the untouched finished portrait is revealed from a
deterministic under-resolved surrogate through global, sweep, band, or
activation fields. The first development pilot omits grain so review isolates
tone, texture, and perceived surface energy.

Earlier motion-rhythm, motion-variable, and White Balance-only renderers remain
available for provenance and research comparison but do not define the active
production direction.

## Candidate assembly

Input:

- one portrait;
- one duration;
- one visual development recipe;
- one compatible audio recipe;
- a random seed and candidate count.

Output:

- silent intermediate video;
- soundtrack intermediate;
- assembled review MP4;
- a combined JSON provenance manifest.

The Candidate Engine validates that audio and video durations agree, coordinates generation, and muxes the result. It stores ratings and decisions supplied by the artist but does not infer or automate them.

## Archive production model

```text
120 archive portraits
├── 40 people age 100 or older
├── 40 people age 12 or younger
└── 40 mixed-age portraits involving the number prop

Visual bank: 5 development candidates per portrait = 600
Audio bank: 50 per duration = 150
Pair bank: 10 options after each visual selection
Final masters: completed visual-and-audio works awaiting human sequencing
Sequence: a versioned custom order of 120 approved masters
Publication ledger: created from the locked sequence
```

Now mode is a future input mode and is outside the current production build.

## Replaceable photo source

A portrait owns the creative assembly, not a particular export of the
photograph. Replacing a photo increments its revision and marks visual/final
renders stale while preserving the selected visual recipe, audio, optional text,
reviews, and any sequence history.

## Deferred episode sequence

An episode number is release order, not asset identity. Approved final videos
are arranged after their visual treatment and audio can be judged together. Draft
sequences may be reordered and versioned. Locking a complete sequence converts
positions 1–120 into episode numbers and creates publication ledger rows.

Lightroom Classic may remain the familiar human sequencing surface. A future
connector or the local review interface commits that custom order to the
Registry; Danny does not manually duplicate it in a spreadsheet.

## Text Plugin boundary

Text remains a nondestructive optional layer. The initial treatment is a minimal “How People Relate” identity element considered only after the audio-video master is selected. Channel-specific necessity remains an editorial decision.

## Human boundary

The artist:

1. chooses which portraits enter production;
2. defines and revises constraints;
3. reviews generated candidates;
4. decides what is successful;
5. selects the published work;
6. changes the system in response to experience.

Automation increases the number of considered possibilities. It does not define quality.

## Reproducibility

Every assembled candidate records:

- portrait path;
- duration;
- finished-source and surrogate checksums, visual recipe, field parameters, and generator version;
- audio recipe, ingredients, and generator version;
- random seed;
- intermediate and final output paths.

The same finished source, configuration, masks, and seeds reproduce the same
surrogate, surface field, and candidate.

## Audio Recipe Library boundary

The existing Google Sheet remains the human authoring surface for the Audio
Recipe Library. Production imports a dated, checksummed, versioned snapshot of
the recipe rows into machine-readable configuration and records that snapshot
ID in audio manifests. The Sheet is not manually mirrored into SQLite: an
import operation creates or updates Registry records, and the prior snapshot
remains reproducible.
